    Rights and facts worth knowing
    
    
    Eligibility for VR does not require legal blindness
    
      
    You qualify for vocational rehabilitation if you have a physical or mental impairment that is a substantial barrier to employment, you can benefit from services, and you need them to get or keep a job. Any visual impairment that gets in the way of work can qualify. Legal blindness (20/200 or worse in the better eye with correction, or a visual field of 20 degrees or less) matters mainly for which unit serves you and for some other programs.
    
      
    If you receive SSI or SSDI you are presumed eligible. The agency only needs to confirm you want to work. This is in 34 CFR 361.42.
    
    
    
    
    Deadlines the agency must meet
    
      
    Eligibility must be decided within 60 days of your application unless you agree in writing to more time. Your Individualized Plan for Employment (IPE) must be written within 90 days of the eligibility decision. These are in 34 CFR 361.41 and 361.45.
    
    
    
    
    Assistive technology skips the "other funding first" search
    
      
    Agencies normally have to look for comparable benefits such as insurance before paying. Federal rules exempt rehabilitation technology, which includes assistive technology devices and services, from that search. This is in 34 CFR 361.53(b). If a counselor tells you to try Medicaid or Medicare first for a braille display, point to this rule.
    
    
    
    
    Financial needs tests and what they can't touch
    
      
    States may ask about your income before paying for some services, including devices. They may not apply a financial test to assessment, counseling, job placement, or to anyone receiving SSI or SSDI. Many states with a separate blind agency have no financial test at all. Ask your counselor directly whether your state applies one to assistive technology.
    
    
    
    
    Informed choice: you help pick the device and vendor
    
      
    You have the right to make informed choices about your services and providers, including which braille display meets your goal. The agency may require quotes and may choose the lowest-cost option that meets the need, but the need is defined by your employment goal, not by the cheapest device on the market. Document why cell count, Bluetooth, notetaker functions, or portability matter for your actual work.
    
    
    
    
    Order of selection and waiting lists
    
      
    When funds are short, a state may serve people in priority categories. People who are blind nearly always fall in the highest category ("most significantly disabled"). If you are told there is a waiting list, ask in writing which category you were placed in and why.
    
    
    
    
    Your free advocate: the Client Assistance Program
    
      
    Every state has a Client Assistance Program (CAP), funded by the federal government and independent of the VR agency. CAP staff explain your rights, help you prepare for meetings, and can represent you in appeals at no cost. Ask the VR agency for the CAP contact; they must give it to you at application and whenever a service is denied.
